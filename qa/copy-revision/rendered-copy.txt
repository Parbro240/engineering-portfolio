Project Overview
This personal project combines my interest in golf club design with CAD and hands-on prototyping. I designed custom mallet putters with a focus on head geometry, weight distribution, and assembly.
Design and Prototyping
I developed several versions in SolidWorks and built physical prototypes using 3D printing and molded components. I use a Bambu P1S for 3D printing.
Working with physical parts helped me consider assembly and manufacturing alongside the CAD geometry. I used repeated prototyping to guide design revisions.
Modular Face and Assembly
I developed a screw-on face concept so the face could be removed and reassembled. I considered fastening methods, heat-set inserts, and printable geometry as part of the design.
I applied Design for Assembly (DFA) and Design for Manufacturability (DFM) principles to the putter and its components.
Current Design
I’ve built multiple physical prototypes and continue to develop the design. My current focus is the putter’s geometry, modular assembly, and manufacturability.
What I Learned
The project has given me practical experience moving between a CAD model and physical parts. It has also helped me connect design decisions with the steps needed to manufacture and assemble a prototype.
Related Research
I’ve started putter face research under Dr. Xiang at Oklahoma State University. I’m studying how face materials and surface finishes affect ball behavior after impact. That work is described in my
Golf Putter Performance Research
case study.

Research Overview
I’ve started putter face research under Dr. Xiang at Oklahoma State University. I want to understand how different face materials and surface finishes affect what a golf ball does immediately after impact.
I’m interested in the ball’s bounce, skid, spin, and transition to true roll. This research builds on my work designing and prototyping custom putters.
What I Want to Compare
How different face materials affect the ball’s initial motion.
How surface finishes change the ball’s behavior after contact.
How those differences relate to bounce, skid, spin, and true roll.
Possible Test Methods
High-speed video and ball tracking could help me study the ball’s motion. Marked golf balls could help track rotation.
A controlled putting or pendulum-style setup could allow comparisons between face materials and surface finishes.
Ongoing Work
As the research progresses, I’ll use this page to share the test setup, measurements, and findings.
